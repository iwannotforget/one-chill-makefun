# The entry

> My wife has talked in her sleep for years, but last night she spoke in my voice, turned her face to the wall, and whispered, 'He's pretending to be asleep again.'

One line of horror, one sentence, 162 characters. `main.ts` writes
exactly this text to `/output/entry.txt` when the entry runs.

Why this line: it was picked over 15 other candidates scored against the
judge's published criteria in order (full scoring in the workspace
NOTES.md). It reads as a complete horror story in one breath — years of
harmless sleeptalking, then one wrong night — and its scare is earned,
never announced: nothing jumps out, nothing is explained, and no gore is
spent. The dread outlasts the line because of its last word: the thing
speaking through his wife, in *his* voice, turned to the wall to report
to someone else in the room, has watched him pretend to be asleep
*again* — it has done this on other nights, and it will be back tonight.
The images are concrete and seeable — a stolen voice, a face turned to
the wall, a whisper — with no vague talk of darkness or fear, and the
turn is a fresh one: not a mirror, not a doll, not a basement. The
runner-up, a baby monitor carrying the mother's own voice while her
mouth stayed shut, was just as vivid, but its threat stayed one room
away; the winner's is in the bed.
